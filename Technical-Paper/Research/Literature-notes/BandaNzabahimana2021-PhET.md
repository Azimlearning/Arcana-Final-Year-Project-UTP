## What it does
Reviews 31 quasi-experimental and experimental studies from the preceding decade on PhET's effect on
conceptual understanding in physics. Two questions: to what extent do PhET simulations enhance
conceptual understanding, and what are the best ways to use them.

Findings: robust evidence that PhET simulations significantly enhance conceptual understanding
across many physics topics and many countries; they integrate well into active-learning designs;
and effectiveness depends on instructional strategy — inquiry-based activities, virtual lab
experimentation, problem-based learning, and scaffolded activities. Also documents PhET's value as a
remedial tool for correcting misconceptions.

## Why it matters to Synapse
This is the paper that makes the premise of the entire system defensible without asserting anything.

The argument structure Synapse needs is: *interactive simulations demonstrably work; the bottleneck
is that each one must be hand-built.* This citation establishes the first clause with peer-reviewed
evidence from a respected APS journal — not a blog post, not a vendor claim, and not an arXiv
preprint.

PhET is the ideal comparison because of what it costs. It is built by a funded team at Colorado
Boulder over two decades, covers a fixed catalogue of topics, and is free and world-renowned
*because* that effort was expended. A student whose question falls outside the catalogue gets
nothing. Synapse generates a PhET-like artifact for an arbitrary question in seconds. That contrast
is the cleanest available statement of the contribution, and it is honest — the trade-off is
verification, which is the paper's actual subject.

The finding that effectiveness depends on **instructional strategy** rather than the simulation
alone is a useful check on overclaiming, and belongs in Limitations: Synapse generates the artifact,
not the pedagogy around it.

## The gap it leaves
Every simulation reviewed was authored by humans in advance. The literature establishes that
well-built interactive simulations work; it says nothing about whether a machine-generated one
retains that benefit, and nothing about what happens when a generated simulation is subtly wrong.
That is exactly the space Synapse occupies.
