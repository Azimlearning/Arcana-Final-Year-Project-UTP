# EduEVAL-DB: A Role-Based Dataset for Pedagogical Risk Evaluation in Educational Explanations

**Authors not confirmed** (related team to AIriskEval-edu: Irigoyen, Daza, Jurado, Fierrez,
Tolosana, Ortigosa et al.) · 2026 · arXiv:2602.15531v1
**Domain:** Risk in AI-generated educational content · **Verified:** 🟡 abstract and rubric confirmed
**Priority:** ⭐ why unverified generation is unacceptable in education specifically

## What it does
854 explanations across 139 ScienceQA questions (science, language, social science, K–12). Each
question has one human-teacher explanation and six generated by LLM-simulated teacher roles designed
to embody real instructional shortcomings — including a **"Confidently Inaccurate Teacher"** that is
fluent and assertive but occasionally factually wrong.

Proposes a five-dimension **pedagogical risk rubric**:

1. **Factual correctness** (epistemic risk)
2. **Explanatory depth and completeness** (pedagogical risk)
3. **Focus and relevance** (cognitive risk)
4. **Student-level appropriateness**
5. **Ideological bias**

Benchmarks Gemini 2.5 Pro against a fine-tuned Llama 3.1 8B; fine-tuning improves every dimension
except factual accuracy, where the frontier model's broader world knowledge wins.

## Why it matters to Synapse
This paper articulates *why education is different* better than anything else in the set, and it
does so in one phrase:

> factual errors represent a risk of **concept corruption, where misconceptions are implanted in the
> learner** — particularly damaging due to the "illusion of truth" effect and the cognitive
> difficulty of unlearning established misconceptions.

That is the justification for the hard rule that no unverified artifact reaches a user. In most
domains a wrong LLM output is an annoyance the user discards. In education it is **persistent
damage** — the student carries the misconception forward, and unlearning is measurably harder than
learning. That asymmetry is what makes a structural delivery gate proportionate rather than
over-engineered.

The rubric is also directly reusable. Its dimensions map onto what a Synapse Verifier should check,
and its grounding in Cognitive Load Theory (the redundancy effect under "focus and relevance")
connects cleanly to the Mayer citation. Two references reinforcing each other reads as a coherent
theoretical frame rather than scattered citation.

## The gap it leaves
Evaluates **static text explanations**. Nothing about interactive artifacts, generated code, or
executable content — where the risk surface is larger (a simulation can be wrong in its dynamics,
not just its words) and the failure is more convincing to the learner, because a running simulation
carries more authority than a paragraph.

## How to cite it
