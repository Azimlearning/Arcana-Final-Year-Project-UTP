## What it does
Extends the Apprentice Tutor Builder with GPT-4 generation so non-technical educators can author
intelligent-tutor interfaces without design training. Generation works at two levels: whole
interface (a starting layout from a high-level description) and individual component (reusable
widgets), deliberately preserving educator control rather than fully automating design. Reports a
preliminary comparison only; the user study is future work.

## Why it matters to Synapse
Historical positioning. It establishes that using LLMs to generate educational interfaces predates
the 2026 wave, and that the field's early instinct was **teacher-facing authoring assistance** —
the same instinct MAIC-UI and the Missing Layer paper still follow two years later.

That is a useful observation for Related Work: across 2024 to 2026, generative UI in education has
been consistently framed as a tool for the educator. Synapse is unusual in pointing it at the
learner directly, and that is worth naming as a positioning choice rather than leaving implicit.

## The gap it leaves
No evaluation of the generated interfaces' quality or pedagogical effect — the authors say so
themselves and defer to a planned study. No verification of correctness at all. And the design
premise assumes the educator is present to judge the output.

## How to cite it
Related Work, one sentence, establishing the pre-2026 origin of the education-GenUI line. Cut it
before cutting MAIC-UI or the Missing Layer paper if space is tight.
