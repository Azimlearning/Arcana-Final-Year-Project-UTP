## What it does
PRISMA 2020 systematic review of PhET simulations in science and chemistry education. Scopus and
Crossref searched to January 2026; 14 empirical studies from 2020–2026 included, spanning elementary
through higher education; risk of bias assessed with an adapted Joanna Briggs Institute tool;
narrative synthesis (heterogeneity precluded meta-analysis).

Findings: PhET simulations consistently improve learning outcomes, most strongly **academic
achievement** (7 studies) and **conceptual understanding** (6 studies). Higher-order skills and
affective outcomes (motivation, attitude) are markedly under-investigated. Effectiveness depends on
instructional design, teacher facilitation and technological accessibility. Concentration of studies
at secondary level is attributed to that being where abstract concepts and sufficient digital
readiness meet.

## Why it matters to Synapse
This is the **recency-compliant** citation for the claim that interactive simulations improve science
learning. Banda & Nzabahimana (2021) covers the same ground with an arguably stronger venue (APS),
but falls outside a four-year window as of August 2026. This does the same job and is four months
old.

It carries three further advantages:

**Mechanism, stated explicitly.** The review attributes effectiveness to visualising abstract
phenomena that cannot be observed directly — atomic interactions, molecular structures — letting
students manipulate variables and see real-time change, and links this to constructivist learning.
That connects cleanly to Mayer's generative-processing construct without needing a separate citation
to bridge them.

**Regional signal.** All six authors are at Universitas Pendidikan Indonesia. For a
Malaysian-hosted, Southeast-Asia-facing conference, citing regional science-education research is
genuine positioning rather than tokenism — and it is a chemistry-education venue, which widens the
disciplinary range of the reference list beyond CS.

**The gap it names is the gap Synapse fills.** Effectiveness "depends on instructional design,
teacher facilitation, and technological accessibility" — all three are human effort per simulation,
and all three are exactly what a generative system changes the economics of.

## The gap it leaves
Fourteen studies, all of the human-authored PhET catalogue. Establishes that carefully built
interactive simulations work; says nothing about generated ones. Also flags that the evidence
concentrates on cognitive outcomes, with higher-order skills and affective outcomes thinly covered —
worth noting if Synapse claims anything about engagement or motivation, because the baseline
evidence there is weaker than for achievement.

## How to cite it
Introduction, first substantive paragraph — the evidence that interactive simulations improve
conceptual understanding in science. Pair with the iJOE 2026 meta-analysis for the effect size
(d = 0.83). Together those two are the whole premise, both from 2026.
