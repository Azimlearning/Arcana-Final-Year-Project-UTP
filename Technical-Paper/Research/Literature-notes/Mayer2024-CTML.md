**Richard E. Mayer** · 2024 · *Educational Psychology Review*
**DOI:** 10.1007/s10648-023-09842-1 (published online 17 Jan 2024)
**Domain:** Learning theory — the pedagogical foundation · **Verified:** ✅ Springer DOI and journal
confirmed
**Priority:** ⭐ the theory citation

## What it does
Mayer's own four-decade retrospective on the Cognitive Theory of Multimedia Learning. Three constant
assumptions: **dual channels** (separate verbal and visual processing), **limited capacity**, and
**active processing** (meaningful learning requires selecting, organizing and integrating). Sets out
the SOI model and 15 evidence-based multimedia design principles, grouped by whether they reduce
extraneous processing, manage essential processing, or foster **generative processing**.

Generative processing is the key construct: cognitive effort aimed at making sense of material,
dependent on the learner's motivation to engage. Mayer describes discovering it precisely when
removing extraneous material stopped being enough to improve learning.

## Why it matters to Synapse
A Track 2 reviewer will ask why an interactive artifact should beat a good text explanation. Without
a theoretical answer, Synapse is a technically interesting system with an unjustified premise.

CTML supplies the answer with a mechanism rather than an assertion: an interactive simulation
engages the visual channel alongside the verbal one (dual channels), and manipulating parameters to
observe consequences is generative processing — the learner actively constructing understanding
rather than receiving it. That is *why* the artifact helps, stated in the vocabulary educational
psychologists use.

Choosing the 2024 retrospective over *Multimedia Learning* (2021) or the original 1990s papers is
deliberate: it is recent, it is a journal article rather than a book, and it is a single citable
source for the whole framework. One reference instead of four.

Note the double edge. CTML's **limited capacity** assumption also predicts that a badly designed
interactive artifact — too many controls, gratuitous animation, decorative detail — actively harms
learning through extraneous processing. That cuts against naive "interactive is better" claims and
should inform both the Generator prompt and an honest Limitations sentence.

## The gap it leaves
CTML assumes carefully designed multimedia produced by humans who applied the principles. It has
nothing to say about **generated** material, where design quality varies per artifact and no one has
verified that the coherence or signalling principles were respected. Whether generated multimedia
satisfies CTML's design principles is an open empirical question — and a good future-work sentence.

## How to cite it
Introduction (why interactive artifacts aid learning) and Limitations (extraneous-processing risk).
Essential for Track 2 credibility — this is the citation that signals the paper takes education
seriously rather than treating it as an application domain.
